The notch, put to work

You hand a task to Claude Code, switch to something else, and come back twenty minutes later to find it's been waiting for a yes since minute two. Visor fixes that. It turns the black strip at the top of your MacBook into a small control room for your AI agents: who's working, who needs you, and how much of your plan is left.

brew install --cask tailamphat/tap/visor

Free and open source · macOS 14 or later · Apple Silicon and Intel · Setup, step by step

A sliver of black that knows who needs you

Closed, Visor is barely there: a little black on each side of the notch. That's enough to tell you everything that matters without opening a thing. Busy agents pulse on the left. When one needs you, the app it's running in appears on the right with a badge: amber to approve, blue to answer. Finished turns get a green check.

Hover, and it opens. Move away, and it's gone.

Claude Code, Copilot CLI and Antigravity CLI.

Say yes without leaving what you're doing

When Claude Code or Copilot CLI asks to run something, the notch opens with who's asking, in which project, and the exact command. Allow it, deny it, or allow it always. "Always" is named after the real command, so you know exactly what you're agreeing to, and a rule for ls never sneaks ls; rm -rf build through.

Claude Code and Copilot CLI. Copilot's "Always" rules are kept per project.

Know you'll run out before you do Next release

The Usage tab already shows every plan you're on: Claude's session and weekly limits, Copilot premium requests on each GitHub account (company ones too), Antigravity's model limits and OpenCode Go. Now each limit also watches how fast you're going. "On pace" means you'll make it to the reset. "Runs out in ~1h 15m" means it's time to slow down.

And when a limit will run out within the hour, the closed notch swaps its percent for the time left, so you see it coming without looking for it.

Claude, Copilot, Antigravity and OpenCode Go. Your own sign-ins, nothing new to log in to.

Every agent, in one list

Three terminals, two editors, and you've lost track of who's doing what. The Agents tab lists every running session, grouped by agent, with what it's doing right now: "Bash: npm test", "Edit: README.md". Click one and the app it runs in comes forward. Restart Visor and the list is still there.

Your clipboard and your music, a hover away

Press ⌘⇧V anywhere for everything you've copied, searchable, and paste it back into the app you were in. Passwords from your password manager are never kept. And whatever's playing, in Spotify, Apple Music or a YouTube tab, shows up with its artwork and the controls you'd expect.

Built to sit there quietly

It's always on screen, so it had better not cost you anything. Visor is native Swift with no web view. It waits for events instead of checking on a timer, and its little animations run in the window server, not on your CPU.

What else is new

Small things that add up, coming in the next release.

  • Done, with a check on top

    A finished turn now shows the agent's mark with a small green check, just like the "?" and "!" badges. The project and how long it took are on hover.

  • Agents remember where they were

    Visor used to forget your running sessions when it restarted, so the list said "No agents running" until each one did something. Now they're right where you left them.

  • Your plan, as it is today

    Upgraded from Pro to Max? The plan name now comes from your account, not from when Claude Code last signed in.

  • "Waiting for your reply" only when it is

    An agent that finishes with a plain answer no longer shows a blue "?" a minute later. Only a turn that ended with a question does.

  • The same breathing room everywhere

    Every icon in the closed notch sits the same distance from the notch and from the edge, whatever it shows. Music next to busy agents is now a small badge on the artwork.

  • A cleaner close

    Moving away quickly could leave a line of text hanging below the notch for a moment. Not anymore.

Make yourself at home

Visor isn't from the App Store, so macOS asks you to approve it once. Here's every prompt you'll see, in order.

  1. Install with Homebrew

    brew install --cask tailamphat/tap/visor

    Already have it? brew update && brew upgrade --cask visor. Homebrew only looks for new versions about once a day, so run brew update first.

  2. Let macOS open it

    Homebrew clears the download flag for you, so Visor usually just opens. If macOS says Apple could not verify it:

    1. Click Done (not Move to Trash).
    2. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security and scroll to Security.
    3. Next to "Visor was blocked to protect your Mac", click Open Anyway, enter your password, then Open.

    If it says Visor "is damaged", remove the download flag in Terminal and open it again:

    xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Visor.app

  3. Connect your agents

    Hover the notch and choose Connect your agents (it's in the menu bar icon too). Visor adds its hook to Claude Code, Copilot CLI and Antigravity so the notch hears what they do. Disconnect from the same menu before uninstalling.

  4. Say yes to the permissions, when they come up

    • Accessibility, for pasting from the clipboard and the media keys: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility, turn on Visor.
    • Automation, for play, pause and skip: click Allow when macOS asks if Visor may control Spotify, Music or your browser.
    • Keychain, for the sign-ins your AI tools already saved: if macOS asks whether security may use "Claude Code-credentials" or "copilot-cli", click Always Allow.

  5. Hover the notch

    Usage fills in from the tools you're signed in to. If one is missing, the gear on the Usage tab says why, with one button to fix it.

Turned something down by mistake? Every permission can be changed in System Settings → Privacy & Security. Uninstall with brew uninstall --cask visor.

Your data stays on your Mac

Visor has no server of its own, no account and no analytics.

  • Your own sign-ins. Claude Code's keychain entry, gh, Copilot CLI and OpenCode, read when needed and never stored.
  • Each tool's own API. Usage comes straight from Anthropic, GitHub and OpenCode. Spend is worked out from Claude Code's logs, on your Mac.
  • A clipboard that stays home. 200 items or 30 days, pins kept. Anything your password manager marks as secret is skipped.
